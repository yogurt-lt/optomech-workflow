# Assembly completeness before delivery

Read this when reviewing a proposed stack or changing a cell, bore, spacer or sleeve. Valid solids, no overlap and a clear insertion path are necessary but do not establish an assembled lens.

Record each lens or permanently cemented optical unit against its actual geometry:

| Function | Evidence to record |
| --- | --- |
| Radial location | Named bore/pilot and engaged axial land, nominal diametral clearance, or a defined bonding alignment fixture and cure retention |
| Axial datum | Named seat and actual contact band at the surface sag; an optical vertex is not a peripheral seat |
| Closure | Retainer or fastening interface, accessible tool and continuous preload path back to the housing |
| Assembly and service | Insertion direction with previously installed parts; closure/removal access and replaceable module boundaries |

A common-motion cell may still need several bore diameters. After merging synchronous groups, check the smallest lens again: in the projection demo a Ø24 lens was axially clamped in a Ø30.3 bore and lacked radial location. A short Ø24.04 pilot repaired the concept; that clearance is a demo choice, not a transferable production fit. A radial-land audit should flag the old geometry and accept the corrected geometry. Sampling a land proves nominal location only; assess its length, tolerances and loads separately.

Keep a spacer clear of unrelated metal steps unless metal load bypass is intentional and every lens is independently retained. Otherwise a retainer can bottom on metal before clamping glass. Check this using actual contact sag and the final bore geometry.

Carry recent review findings into the next delivery:

- Give spacer ends finite contact bands, radial location and adequate section at axial/diagonal roots. Do not replace a difficult contact with a tiny hook, knife edge or an unlocated ring. A tapered bore may improve aperture clearance, but its ends must still seat.
- Compare close-pair rim contact, a bonded locating seat and a serviceable cell before forcing a miniature spacer into the gap. Preserve optical vertices/air gaps and identify newly manufactured glass rims and any optical-zone edits.
- Use a finite aperture land or independently retained blackened foil. A thin stop foil must not carry the glass stack preload.
- Shorten large sleeves only after reviewing every zoom/focus extreme, front aperture envelope, guide engagement, groove end web and thrust interfaces.
- Generate the section and CAD from the same final parameters. List radial pilot, axial seat and closure for every optical unit; unresolved support blocks an assembly-ready claim even if insertion and Boolean checks pass.

Separate automated numerical results, explicit engineering review and target-CAD execution in the report. Do not describe sampled envelopes as complete 3D cam contact, optical clearance or production qualification.
