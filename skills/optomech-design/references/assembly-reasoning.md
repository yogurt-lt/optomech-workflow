# Choosing the assembly

Use actual curved contacts, source mechanical diameters and available machining access. A radius ordering is a loading candidate, not proof of an assembly path.

| Situation | Useful candidate | Questions to resolve |
|---|---|---|
| Diameters narrow continuously toward one end | One-side-loaded stepped bore and spacers | Does every lens, ring and tool pass through the earlier bores? Does a spacer accidentally bottom on the barrel before touching the next lens? |
| Small waist, larger groups at both ends | Opposed stacks about a central seat | Are both innermost groups independently seated? Does the stop interrupt either path? |
| Long deep bore with close, changing group diameters | Short bench-assembled cells inside one structural barrel | Can cells share a centering OD and carry preload through metal ends? Can the glass be centered and cured before insertion? |
| Very close peripheral glass edges | Revised non-optical bearing rim, independent bonded seat, or a local cell | Is a new blank allowed? Is there real rim thickness and a useful contact band? Can adhesive be applied, cured and inspected? |
| Large aperture design with several functional groups | A few enclosing barrel subassemblies with internal supports | Avoid leaving an exposed chain of threaded cells as the structural housing. Check all pilot roots and closures. |

## Contacts, spacers and rims

For a planar spacer end, determine the contact-compatible plane from the actual face profile over its intended radial band. A spherical face normally contacts on a circle; a nominal band width is not the same as a physical area contact. Record the contact radius and flag later contact chamfer/preload work.

A rectangular spacer is often enough. Use a tapered bore or cup if it removes interference while leaving substantial stock. Do not add narrow hooks merely to avoid clipping in a demo whose user explicitly waived aperture checks. Conversely, an aperture waiver is not permission to change the prescription's central sag or group spacing silently.

Rim changes must include usable axial thickness and centering lands. Extend paired diameters locally instead of giving every lens an oversized common flange. Distinguish peripheral edge revisions from optical redesign and describe the new manufacturing requirement.

## Retention and adhesive

Adhesive holds a part after the fixture/seat establishes position; it does not magically establish centering or eliminate stress. Specify accessible local bond sites, clearances as assumptions, cure restraint and inspection. Do not assume a glue material, shrinkage, CTE match or qualified bond thickness.

Use mechanical spacers when there is space and a clear loading path. Keep isolated bonded cells where they solve a real close-edge or bench-alignment problem. If compression should bypass glass, trace it through metal end faces and check that both faces actually contact the neighboring parts.

## Structural continuity

Inspect meridional sections at counterbore steps and barrel joints. Thick radial walls can still be linked by a thin axial or diagonal web if inner and outer steps nearly cross. Check the distance between opposite wall chains as well as the remaining stock around holes/slots. Move the pilot radius, move its axial split or keep a continuous outer wall when that gives a simpler load path. Add practical chamfers/fillets only where they do not remove the contact datum.

## Appropriate evidence

Primary examples already used in this project:

- [Edmund: Integration of Optical Systems](https://www.edmundoptics.eu/knowledge-center/application-notes/optics/integration-of-optical-systems/) — sag at a chosen contact radius affects seats and spacers.
- [Olympus JP3850240B2](https://patents.google.com/patent/JP3850240B2/en) — microscope optical groups in holding frames; relevant to short-cell preassembly.
- [Olympus US6424473B1](https://patents.google.com/patent/US6424473B1/en) — fixtures, positioning and bonding in assembly.
- [Lensrentals: Rokinon 24 mm teardown](https://www.lensrentals.com/blog/2013/05/first-look-rokinon-24mm-f3-5-tilt-shift-part-2/) — first-hand modular front/rear optical assemblies and shims.
- [Lensrentals: Canon 35 mm II teardown](https://www.lensrentals.com/blog/2015/12/canon-35mm-f1-4-mk-ii-teardown/) — enclosing photographic barrel construction.
- [Arizona: Athermal Bonded Mounts](https://wp.optics.arizona.edu/optomech/wp-content/uploads/sites/53/2016/10/Slides_Monti_Athermal-Bonded-Mounts.pdf) — bond and thermal mismatch need explicit design.

Read the relevant source before applying a niche detail. Do not imply that a cited source validates the demo's dimensions, loading force, tolerances or material selection.
