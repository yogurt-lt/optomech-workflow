# Lessons from six reviewed concepts

These describe the final accepted architecture, not universal recipes. Values belong to their demo scale.

- **Fixed projection PF-2:** close first edges made tiny spacers unattractive. Three independently seated/bonded lenses replaced the tiny front stack; only one blank gained a mechanical rim. Separate rear stacks retained spacers and rings.
- **Film projection:** the largest element was not the first lens. A small first-lens adapter allowed the rest to load through one opening. The baseline ray report found contact-band clipping; execution success did not resolve that optical issue.
- **63X microscope:** five short optical cells were assembled and bonded on a bench, then loaded into one enclosing tube. Metal ends carried the common preload. Prescription direction faced the specimen at +Z, so reading “first group” as “front specimen group” would have reversed the assembly description. STOP on a curved face was not turned into an arbitrary flat plate.
- **Phone 6P:** large common flanges on all lenses were unnecessary. Three bore stages, local paired rim extensions and rectangular spacers simplified the structure. The tail ring bonded to the housing; it did not imply bonding every glass surface. Injection-molding details remained outside the demo.
- **SLR:** early exposed cells and excessive adhesive were revised into three enclosing structural barrels. Large gaps used substantial spacers; local cells remained only where useful. A later radial-wall check missed narrow axial/diagonal bridges at pilot and counterbore roots. Reworked continuous walls fixed the geometry; checking opposite wall-chain distance became part of the workflow.
- **Wide angle:** two barrels used three simple spacers. The close G1/G2 pair gained a direct peripheral bearing contact. The second barrel loaded from both sides of its stop; its front ring had to be secured before mating the barrels. The stop bevel went on the image side because the upstream glass left insufficient space for an upstream bevel.

When a user rejects a concept as difficult to machine or assemble, change the architecture as needed. Do not just report that the sampled section has no overlap. Document what changed, why, and which actual CAD/runtime and manufacturing checks remain.
