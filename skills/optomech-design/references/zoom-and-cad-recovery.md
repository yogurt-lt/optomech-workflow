# Zoom mechanisms and CAD recovery

Read all optical configurations before selecting a cam. Determine which gaps change, identify cemented groups, and match exported STEP coordinates to the correct configuration; base DISZ can represent another configuration. Preserve rounding differences explicitly instead of silently moving the fixed groups. Source focal-length comments are labels until the optical solver verifies EFL.

Define the fixed, rotating and translating bodies. Separate precision guidance/load support from cam drive. Check reversing compensation curves, slot intersections, roller diameter, remaining webs and independent travel stops. Repeated120-degree grooves can intersect even when a single groove works. Check the complete group envelope including retainers, optical cells and revised rims at intermediate positions, not just source endpoints. Sampling is not continuous-motion or tolerance certification.

Consider two-end loading around an integral stop before adding another lens cell. A cell is useful when it resolves access, preload or adjustment; explain the reason. When strengthening a step root, inspect axial and diagonal material connections as well as radial walls. Added lens rims require newly manufactured blanks and must be clearly distinguished from supplied optics. A thicker front retainer can strike the preceding moving group mid-travel; a design that fits only at endpoints is insufficient.

For large SolidWorks macros, save and close completed standalone parts, then preload/insert one at a time and close their separate windows. Referenced geometry remains in assembly memory. Preserve saved accepted parts for a local resume, verifying identity and final geometry version. Do not distribute workstation-specific paths in a public macro. Repeated failures at a fixed document count suggest resource pressure but do not prove its cause; inspect logs and resource state.

Reject consecutive near-duplicate profile points in physical units before calling CAD sketch APIs. Floating-point radii such as25.800999999999995 and25.801mm can produce an unusable line. Pick a tolerance consistent with the model's units and CAD resolution; preserve intentional small edges. Check every closed profile including its last-to-first edge and log the failing segment.

For long cam cuts, validate body count after critical operations. A feature object existing is not evidence of a valid single solid. Cross-kernel loft correspondence/smoothing can differ. Short adjacent-section cuts are a useful recovery diagnostic, with substantial feature-count cost; prefer verified efficient sweeps/lofts or patterns for the maintained implementation. Save a diagnostic part at the first body split; do not silently discard fragments. Do not infer SolidWorks execution from a successful FreeCAD build.

For transparent zoom demonstrations, use full final CAD meshes with their true holes, slots and transforms. Rotate cams about their own axes and pinions about their offset centers; translate optical groups using the reviewed motion law. Transparency is an illustration setting. Provide pause/scrub, opacity and orbit controls; distinguish the mechanical animation from optical imaging, refraction, autofocus calibration and stress simulation. Label the exact endpoint ratio when a rounded marketing name is used.

For optics-focused viewers, keep the complete housing transparent while giving moving optical cells stronger contrast than the static shell. Use consistent group colors across glass, cells, motion plots and annotations; separately distinguish the cam-track colors. Provide all/mechanics-only/optics-only modes that preserve the current angle. Exclude drive overlays and fasteners from optics-only mode. Keep guide rods visible at a moderate independent opacity and show slot boundaries plus a live unwrapped cam diagram when the cylindrical view obscures transmission. Treat opacity and initial pose as adjustable presentation settings, not mechanical-design constraints.
